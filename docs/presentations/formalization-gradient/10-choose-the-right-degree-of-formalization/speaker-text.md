# FG08 — Choose the right degree of formalization

The meta-rule is to make the implementation choice visible. For a recurring readiness assessment, we can compare an interpretative procedure, a hybrid skill with validators, and a more executable workflow.

The interpretative approach is easy to revise and gives the agent room to consider context. Its mechanical consistency depends on how the procedure is followed. The hybrid approach separates contextual judgment from stable checks. It introduces a new responsibility: keeping the layers aligned. An executable workflow makes implemented checks repeatable, but exceptions and policy changes require software maintenance.

None of these options automatically establishes correctness. A program can implement a mistaken policy consistently. A formal operation can also depend on changing inputs or model output. That is why formalization, determinism and correctness must remain separate concepts.

For an important architectural choice, the agent should present concrete alternatives, explain their costs and recommend one with reasons. We can then choose the degree of formalization appropriate to the risk, repetition and maturity of the rule. There is no requirement to turn every conversation into code.
