# Slide 03 — Explanation and example

A small example makes the distinction clearer. “Show a warning when I talk too much” is a plausible user story, but it leaves several decisions open. Does a brief pause end a speaking turn? Can the user change the threshold? Should the application suppress a warning when speaker identity is uncertain?

A useful specification answers the decisions that matter for this increment, or marks them as unresolved. It need not be a long document. An example with a beginning, an expected warning, and an exception may be more useful than several pages of general language.

The choice is therefore not between agility and specifications. Our proposed discipline is to keep specifications proportionate and revisable, and to link them to checks and feedback. We should not introduce a document merely because a template exists, or dismiss one merely because its content is written rather than executable.

A good test is whether another person or an agent can use the artifact to make the intended decision without guessing. When the answer is no, the remedy may be a clearer example, a definition, or a conversation whose decision is then recorded.
