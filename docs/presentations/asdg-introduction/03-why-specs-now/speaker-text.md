# Slide 3 — Why Specs Now?

We have just seen the lifecycle and the humans and agents working together. The natural question is: why bring specifications and project structure back into the foreground?

This slide takes a quick historical view. Traditional development put substantial effort into requirements and design before implementation. That gave teams clarity and traceability, but the documents could be heavy and hard to change. Agile challenged that cost. It rightly emphasized working software and frequent feedback. Many teams kept only the documentation they found useful. This picture simplifies a varied history; Agile never prohibited design or specifications.

Now agents change the conditions again. They can help create and update architecture, flows, tests, and explanations. They also need explicit context before they can work responsibly. A human colleague might remember months of conversations; an agent cannot read our minds. Intent, use cases, constraints, and quality criteria must be discoverable in the project.

The bottom of the slide says that specifications become a source of truth. I mean a source of intended behavior and decisions that agents and humans can share. We still have to check that intent against code, tests, and what happens in production. The next slide shows why maintaining this knowledge has become more practical.
