# Slide 12 — Visual walkthrough

The large comparison is between learning and operating. On the left, three instructions define a useful experiment: begin with an architectural question, build only enough to test it, and keep the learning. The small descriptions ask what is uncertain after high-level design, require a success criterion and evidence, and allow the design to change with the result.

The purple proof-of-concept panel expands that into four activities: explore one uncertainty, implement quickly, check the success criterion, and record the learning. Its outcome is labelled Learn. The blue Production panel asks for agreed behavior, quality and security checks, human review and acceptance, and deployment, observation, and support. Its outcome is Ship and Operate.

The slogan “vibe equals proof of concept” expresses how we want to use exploratory speed. It does not mean that any quickly generated code automatically constitutes a valid experiment.

The footer fixes the order: step five proposes the architecture, and step six tests a risky assumption. Later stages establish whether the resulting change is ready for people to use. Passing a narrow experiment cannot stand in for all of those later commitments.
