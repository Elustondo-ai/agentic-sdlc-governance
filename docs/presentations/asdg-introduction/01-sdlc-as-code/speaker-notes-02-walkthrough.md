# Slide 01 — Visual walkthrough

Let us read the complete diagram, starting at the top and moving clockwise. Step one is Context and Domain Analysis: understand the situation, the people involved, and the domain in which the problem exists. Step two is Intent: define the goals, desired outcomes, and principles. Context describes the situation; intent says what we want to change.

Step three is System Model and Use Cases: identify actors, scenarios, and expected behavior. Step four is Benchmarks, Test Strategy and Success Criteria: decide how to compare alternatives, what evidence to collect, and what success would mean. These come before implementing our preferred solution.

Step five is High-Level Solution Design and Architecture: shape components, boundaries, and trade-offs. Step six is Viable Proof of Concept: test an important assumption that remains uncertain. Step seven is Planning: organize the increment, tasks, risks, and evidence. Step eight is Low-Level Design: specify precise behavior and contracts, including interfaces, schemas, states, and rules.

Step nine is the Build and Test Cycle: iterate on implementation, tests, and evidence. Step ten is Document, Review and Release: make the change understandable so the human can review and approve the code, ready for end-to-end testing. Step eleven is Operational Reality, DevOps and User Acceptance: test the change in a running system. Step twelve is Observability, Insights and Learning: use the resulting evidence to improve the next cycle's context.

The central circle keeps humans, AI, and agents together. Judgment, creativity, reasoning, alignment, and accountability belong inside the process, not outside it. The right-hand panel adds rules and policies, quality gates, security and privacy, and compliance across the cycle. It is not a final checkpoint after all the work. The left-hand panel says to observe, learn, adapt, and improve; its return arrow makes learning an input to the next decision.

Finally, the two strips beneath the circle show supporting capabilities: agents, interfaces and applications; knowledge, data and graphs; and a repository connecting code, specifications, evidence, and controls. Models, security, communication, and platform infrastructure support that work. They are not extra lifecycle stages. The clockwise arrow describes a logical progression, with feedback rather than a claim that real projects never revisit earlier decisions.
