# Slide 10 — Explanation and example

Imagine a roadmap outcome of helping users communicate more effectively. A first release might concentrate on simple speaking-time feedback. A sprint within that release could explore how one warning is shown and dismissed. These are illustrative scopes, not an approved Speaker Coach plan.

The sprint should reuse the relevant release intent and architecture. It records only the design changes and evidence needed for its narrower question. When the sprint finishes, its findings may suggest that the release needs a different warning mechanism or a smaller scope.

A status record prevents an agent from confusing “implementation tests passed” with “human review finished” or “release accepted.” Those are different states, supported by different evidence.

The practical rule is to connect the levels without copying them indiscriminately. Refer to the governing context, state what this increment changes, and preserve the evidence that could alter the wider plan.
