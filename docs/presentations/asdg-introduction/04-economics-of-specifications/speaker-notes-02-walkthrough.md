# Slide 04 — Visual walkthrough

The visual has three columns: Before AI, The Shift, and With AI. On the left, documentation is expensive to create and maintain, becomes outdated, is rarely consumed, and is disconnected from code. The result box calls that high cost and low value. These are problems the slide is trying to address, not inevitable properties of all earlier documentation.

In the middle, human intent passes through AI into working artifacts. The six qualities below it are readable, transformable, searchable, summarizable, refinable, and verifiable. Readable means people and tools can use the material. Transformable means it can guide tests, implementation, or other explanations. Searchable and summarizable make relevant context easier to retrieve. Refinable means feedback can improve it. Verifiable means that important claims connect to checks and evidence.

On the right, the corresponding opportunities are cheaper generation, cheaper maintenance, continuing relevance, active consumption, and connections to other engineering artifacts. Developers, project managers, quality reviewers, and agents can use the same knowledge. The result box calls this low cost and high value, but the diagram supplies no benchmark proving that outcome for every project.

The dark strip at the bottom brings the argument together: specifications can guide tests and code, feed agent context and search, improve through feedback, and remain shared between people and tools. “Operational” does not mean every sentence executes itself. The final footer preserves the purpose: better systems, humans and AI working together, and an ambition for faster, safer progress.
