# Slide 04 — Explanation and example

Our presentation workflow is a concrete example of part of this idea. We keep the visual and narration as source artifacts, and a shared builder assembles a viewable deck. A change to the source can be carried into a new presentation without manually editing a separate PowerPoint master.

But notice what that automation does not establish. A successful build says that the artifact was assembled. It does not prove that a diagram is accurate, that its narration explains enough, or that a listener understands it. Your son's feedback is evidence of a gap that mechanical packaging checks did not detect.

This is why creation cost and trustworthy maintenance must remain separate. Adding more generated paragraphs is inexpensive; choosing the right explanation, checking its coverage, and keeping it aligned with the visual takes judgment.

The improvement we are making is therefore specific: retain the existing introduction, add a walkthrough of the visual's meaningful content, and add examples where they remove uncertainty. That is a targeted refinement of operational knowledge, not a claim that all documentation becomes valuable simply because AI produced it.
