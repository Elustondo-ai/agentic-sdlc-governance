# Slide 4 — AI Changed the Economics of Specifications

The earlier slide raised the historical question. Here is the economic change behind it. When people had to write, draw, update, and reconcile every document by hand, even useful documentation could become costly. It would drift as the software changed. Teams then had good reasons to keep it lean.

AI can now help draft an architecture diagram, turn a use case into test ideas, summarize a design for a reviewer, or find a contradiction between an implementation and a specification. It can help update related artifacts when the project changes. Creating text is cheap; maintaining trustworthy knowledge still needs a real review process. I would not read the words "cheap" and "continuously relevant" on this slide as automatic guarantees.

The center of the visual is the critical move: human intent becomes working artifacts that people and agents can both use. The value grows when those artifacts live near the code, are versioned, guide agent behavior, connect to tests, and can be checked against runtime evidence. In that sense, some specifications become operational context. They remain human-readable documents too.

This economics argument gives us an opportunity. But cost alone does not explain why SDLC2 matters. Next, I want to show the necessity: agents need context that may currently be scattered or only held in people's heads.
