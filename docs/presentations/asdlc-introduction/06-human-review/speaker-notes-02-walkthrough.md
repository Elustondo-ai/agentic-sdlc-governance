# Slide 06 — Visual walkthrough

Follow the two arrows across this slide. The left panel separates three kinds of agent work: code and design, tests and quality assurance, and review and risks. The caption warns that many artifacts can arrive quickly while human attention remains finite.

The central review package answers three questions. What changed should identify the affected behavior and components. How was it verified should point to actual tests, checks, and relevant results. What needs a decision should make unresolved trade-offs, limitations, and approval boundaries visible.

The line underneath specifies the form: short text, a component diagram, tests, and links to detail. Short does not mean hiding the evidence. It means giving the reviewer a route through it rather than handing over an undifferentiated pile of files.

The right-hand side names the human activities: understand, question, and approve or redirect. The last line assigns ownership precisely. The agent prepares the explanation; the human examines the evidence and decides. A polished walkthrough does not grant approval and does not substitute for a deeper review where the risk requires it.
