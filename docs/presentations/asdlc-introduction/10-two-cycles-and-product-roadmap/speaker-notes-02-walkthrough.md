# Slide 10 — Visual walkthrough

The nested shapes on the left show scope. The outer rectangle is the product roadmap: direction, sequence, and outcomes. Inside it, the release circle represents a milestone and its twelve-step cycle. The smaller sprint circle represents a focused increment and its own twelve-step cycle.

The table on the right says what to record at each level. The roadmap holds purpose, priorities, and outcomes. A release holds milestone, scope, and readiness. A sprint holds focused intent, its build work, and its evidence. This is not a requirement to duplicate the same document at all three levels.

The governance-status box makes the current position explicit: active release, active sprint, current stage, and review state. The paired arrows beneath it explain the information flow. Context moves inward from the longer-range direction to focused work. Evidence moves outward from that work to inform release and roadmap decisions.

Finally, read the two lines at the bottom together. In the convention used by this presentation, step eleven at sprint scope means human acceptance in development or staging. At release scope, it means human acceptance in production. The roadmap guides these recurring cycles; it is not another mandatory twelve-step cycle wrapped around them.
